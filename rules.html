RULES OF PLAY

The Classic

The rules that govern The Classic. Anything not covered here is decided by the standard Rules of Golf, and any question of play is settled by the two captains.

1

The Competition

1.1
Team match play. The Classic is a team competition between Team Red (Captain Gavin) and Team Blue (Captain Bel). Every player is drafted to one team. Teams are 6 v 6 (three pairs each), or 8 v 8 (four pairs) if the field grows. Pairs play 2 v 2 matches against the other team's pairs. The front nine is played as a scramble, the back nine as alternate shot (Rule 4).
1.2
Captains. Each team has a captain who sets the lineup, enters or oversees scoring, and rules on any question of play (Rule 9). Captains also play, and set the pairings.
1.3
The draft. Players are seeded and drafted in snake order (Red, Blue, Blue, Red, …). Captains are not draft picks. A player competes only for the team that drafted them.
1.4
Groups. Each group is one 2 v 2 match — a Red pair against a Blue pair — and has its own scorer. All groups tee off together.
1.5
A substitute may be used only with both captains' agreement. A substitute takes the original player's place in the pair for that match.
2

Points & Winning

2.1
Three points per match. Each match is worth 3 points — one for the front nine, one for the back nine, and one for the overall 18 holes. Each is scored as a separate contest.
2.2
Winning a point. The pair that wins more holes over the front nine wins that point; likewise the back nine. The overall point goes to the pair that wins more holes across all 18.
2.3
A halved point. If the holes are split evenly in a contest, it is halved and each pair earns ½ point.
2.4
Team total. A team's score is the sum of every point its pairs earn across all matches (3 points per match: 9 in all at 6 v 6, 12 at 8 v 8).
2.5
Winning The Classic. The team with the most total points wins.
If the team totals are level, the Classic is shared — unless the captains agree to a playoff (e.g. captain vs captain, or a nominated group replaying a hole).
2.6
Live scoring. Points are shown on the live leaderboard as the front, back and overall contests are decided. A match need not reach the 18th hole (Rule 6.4).
3

Seeds — No Handicap Strokes

3.1
No handicap strokes. No strokes are given in any match. The lower score on a hole wins it.
3.2
Your pop number is your seed for the year, set before the draft. It is used only to order the draft and balance the teams; it does not change during play.
4

Playing a Hole

4.1
Front nine — scramble. Both partners tee off. The pair chooses the best shot, and both partners play their next stroke from within one club-length of that spot, no nearer the hole (on the green, from the marked spot). Repeat until the ball is holed. The pair posts one score.
4.2
Back nine — alternate shot. Partners play one ball and take turns hitting it. One partner tees off on the odd-numbered holes and the other on the even-numbered holes; the pair decides who is which before the back nine begins. After that the partners alternate every stroke until the ball is holed, including putts and penalty strokes.
4.3
Penalties in alternate shot. A penalty stroke is added to the pair's score but does not change whose turn it is to hit. If a ball is replayed from the previous spot, the partner who hit it originally plays it again.
4.4
Par 3s. The same format applies on par 3s: both partners tee off in the scramble, and the designated partner tees off in alternate shot.
4.5
No outside help. No advice beyond your own group, and no assisted shots.
4.6
Wrong ball. Play your own ball. If a pair plays the wrong ball it loses the hole; put it right and continue.
4.7
Equipment. Distance devices (rangefinders, GPS, watches) are allowed.
5

The Course

5.1
Tees. Play the Silver tee boxes.
5.2
Preferred lies (lift, clean & place). Through the green you may lift, clean and place your ball within one club-length, no nearer the hole.
aYou may not improve your lie from rough to fairway, out of a bunker, or from fringe to green.
bThe captains may extend or suspend preferred lies for the day before play begins (for example, allowing placing in bunkers if it has rained).
5.3
Out of bounds & lost ball. For pace, drop within two club-lengths of where the ball crossed out of bounds or was likely lost — in the nearest fairway or rough, no nearer the hole — for a one-stroke penalty, and play on. (Stroke-and-distance is not required.)
5.4
Penalty areas (water). One-stroke penalty. Drop within two club-lengths of where the ball last crossed the margin, no nearer the hole, or replay from the previous spot.
5.5
Bunkers. Play the ball as it lies unless preferred lies in bunkers were called that day (Rule 5.2b). Rake when finished.
5.6
Unplayable lie. One-stroke penalty; drop within two club-lengths, no nearer the hole.
5.7
Cart path & casual relief. Free relief from cart paths, sprinkler heads, casual water and ground under repair — one club-length, no nearer the hole.
6

Match Play & Scoring

6.1
Winning a hole. The pair with the lower score wins the hole. Equal scores halve the hole.
6.2
Concessions & gimmes. A pair may concede a hole, or the opposing pair's next stroke (a "gimme"), at any time. A conceded putt counts as holed. A concession cannot be withdrawn.
6.3
Pick up when you're out of it. Once your pair can no longer win or halve a hole, pick up — the hole is lost. This keeps pace and matches the way the leaderboard scores holes.
6.4
Closing out a nine. A nine (or the overall 18) is decided once a pair leads by more holes than remain in it. Play on: holes still count toward the overall point even after a nine is decided. Once the front, back and overall are all decided, the remaining holes need not be played and the scorer may close the match out.
6.5
Reporting scores. Report each hole to your group's scorer as you go. The live leaderboard updates as scores are entered.
6.6
A hole in doubt. If a hole's result is disputed and can't be settled on the spot, play it as a half, note it, and let the captains resolve it (Rule 9).
7

Mulligans

7.1
One per nine. Each pair gets one mulligan on the front nine and one on the back nine, used by either partner.
7.2
How it works. A mulligan is a re-hit of the previous stroke with no penalty. The re-hit ball must be played — you cannot keep the better of the two.
7.3
Where. A mulligan may be used on any full stroke — a tee shot or a stroke through the green — but not on a putt.
7.4
No carry-over. A pair's unused mulligan does not carry to the other nine or bank for later.
8

Pace & Conduct

8.1
Keep up. Stay within reach of the group ahead. Play ready golf and be prepared to hit when it's your turn.
8.2
Search time. Give a lost ball no more than three minutes before taking relief under Rule 5.3.
8.3
Respect the course & each other. Repair pitch marks, replace divots, rake bunkers, and keep it friendly — it's still a competition.
9

Rulings & Disputes

9.1
Captains decide. Any question of play is settled by the two captains together. If they can't agree, the standard Rules of Golf apply.
9.2
When in doubt, play on. If a ruling can't be made on the spot, note the situation, finish the hole, and resolve it afterward rather than holding up play.
9.3
Spirit of the game. These rules exist to keep matches fair and fast. Where a rule is silent, do the fair, obvious thing.
The Classic — Rules of Play. Subject to revision by the captains before the event.
